Privacy Policy

Stop for a Verse · Last updated 2026-11-14

Stop for a Verse has no accounts and no ads, and the app contains no analytics, crash-reporting or tracking software. Your topics, reminder times, likes and notes are kept on your phone, and we do not receive them unless you choose to send them to us, for example in a problem report. The app itself sends something from your phone only in a few cases: when you open, buy, restore or manage Premium; if you have Premium, when it checks that your subscription is still active; when you open a whole chapter on Bible Gateway; when you email us; and when you share a verse. Your phone’s own backups can also include the app’s data. This page explains each of those in plain words.

Who we are

Stop for a Verse (“the app”) is published by Activley Oy Ab (Business ID 3348464-6), Vinkelvägen 8, 68630 Jakobstad, Finland. We are the controller of the small amount of personal data described on this page.

If you have a question about your privacy, write to activley@proton.me. A real person reads every message.

What stays on your phone

To work, the app saves a few things in its own private storage on your phone. The app does not send this data to us or to anyone else, apart from the cases described under “What leaves your phone” below and your phone’s own backups, and we cannot see it:

  • the topics you chose and the order you chose them in;
  • whether reminders are on, and the times you picked;
  • the verses you liked;
  • the notes you wrote on verses;
  • whether “Learn from my likes” is on. When it is, the app suggests verses by looking at your likes, and that happens entirely on your phone;
  • which verses have already been sent as reminders, so you are not sent the same one again too soon, and the date until which reminders are planned;
  • small things such as whether you have finished setting up the app and which tips you have seen;
  • if you buy Premium: your plan, whether it is active, when it renews or ends, and when it was last checked. The RevenueCat software in the app (see below) also keeps a copy of your subscription status and a random customer number on the phone.

If the app ever finds its saved data damaged, it repairs it and keeps up to five copies of the earlier saved data on the phone, so nothing you wrote is lost. Because of this, such a copy can still hold the text of a note you later deleted. These copies stay on the phone, like the rest, and are removed when you uninstall the app or clear its storage.

If you use the app in a web browser, the same information is kept in that browser’s local storage instead, and is removed when you clear the site’s data.

Reminders are made on your phone

Verse reminders are local notifications. The app plans them on your phone for the times you chose, up to two weeks ahead. They do not come from a server, and the app does not create or send a push token.

The app asks for permission to show notifications when you turn reminders on, save your settings or send a test reminder. On Android, the app can also ask you to allow “Alarms & reminders”, so that reminders arrive exactly at your chosen times instead of up to an hour later; this is optional and lets the app set precise reminder times, nothing more. It does not use your camera, contacts, location or microphone, and it never reads your photos. On iPhone, if you choose to save a verse picture to your photos from the share sheet, iPhone asks once whether the app may add pictures to your photo library; this only adds the picture you chose and does not let the app see your other photos.

A reminder shows the verse and its reference. Depending on your phone’s settings, it can be visible on the lock screen. You can change that in your phone’s notification settings.

Your phone’s own backups

Like most apps, the app’s data can be included in the backups your phone makes for you: Android backup to your Google account, iCloud Backup, or a backup to your computer. These backups belong to you and are handled by Google or Apple under the settings and terms of your own account. The app never uploads anything for backup itself, and we cannot see these backups.

What leaves your phone, and when

Information leaves your phone only in the situations below.

Downloading the app

You get the app from the App Store or Google Play. Apple or Google handle your download, account and payment details under their own privacy policies. We receive reports from them about downloads and sales, such as numbers per country, which we use for our accounts. We do not use them to find out who you are.

If your phone settings allow it, Apple and Google also share anonymous usage statistics and crash reports with us, such as how often the app is opened or where it stopped working. We use them to fix problems and improve the app. You can turn this sharing off in your phone’s privacy or Google Play settings.

Premium purchases

If you buy Premium, Apple or Google take the payment. We never see your card or bank details. The store confirms the purchase with a receipt (Apple) or a purchase token (Google), which the app passes to RevenueCat to check that your subscription is active.

RevenueCat, Inc. is a company in the United States that provides this subscription check for us. It acts on our behalf, as our data processor. The app gives RevenueCat no name, email address or other detail about you: RevenueCat creates a random customer number for your phone. It receives that number, the store receipt or purchase token, the time of your purchases and renewals, the type of device and its operating system, the version of the app and of RevenueCat’s software, your store country, language setting and currency, and, as with any internet connection, the IP address of your phone.

RevenueCat also turns this data into subscription statistics for us, such as how many people subscribe, renew or cancel. We use them to run and improve Premium, not to find out who you are.

The app contacts RevenueCat when you open Premium in the app (to show the current prices), when you buy, restore or manage a subscription, and afterwards while the app stays open. If Premium is saved on your phone, for example because you subscribed or a backup brought it back, the app also checks your subscription when it starts and each time you return to it. If you never open Premium or use “Restore purchases” or “Manage subscription”, the app does not contact RevenueCat.

Reading a whole chapter on Bible Gateway

In a verse’s context, “Read the full chapter” opens the chapter on Bible Gateway in your browser. The link contains only the book and chapter, for example John 14. Once the page opens, you are on Bible Gateway’s website, run by HarperCollins Christian Publishing in the United States. It uses cookies and its own privacy notice applies. Nothing about you is passed to it by the app.

Reporting a problem

In Settings, “Report a problem” opens your own email app with a message to activley@proton.me. Nothing is sent until you press Send, and you can change or delete anything in the message first. To help us find the problem, the message includes, below a space for your own words:

  • a subject line with the app version and build, and your phone’s operating system and its version (for example “Android 15”), which are repeated in the message;
  • whether reminders are on, off, or on but blocked in your phone’s settings;
  • your reminder times, the date until which reminders are planned, and the topics you chose (or “all”);
  • whether you have Premium, and its state (for example active or ended).

It does not include your phone model, your likes, your notes, or any identifier. When you send it, we receive your email address, your name if your email app adds it, what you write, and anything you attach. Our mailbox is with Proton (Proton AG, Switzerland), so Proton stores these emails under its own terms and privacy policy. Switzerland is recognised by the EU as giving personal data adequate protection.

Sharing a verse

“Share text” and “Share image” open your phone’s own share menu with the verse, its reference and the app’s name, as text or as a picture. Your notes and likes are never included. The app sends nothing itself: the app or person you choose receives what you share, under their own terms. A picture is made as a temporary file on your phone and is removed afterwards.

What we do not do

  • We have no user accounts and no sign-in.
  • We show no ads and use no advertising ID.
  • The app contains no analytics, crash-reporting or tracking software, and we do not track you across other apps or websites. The only statistics we see are the ones Apple, Google and RevenueCat provide, as described above.
  • We do not sell your personal data or share it for advertising.
  • We do not build a profile of you. Topic labels on verses are chosen by our editors, and “Learn from my likes” works only on your phone.
  • We do not run our own server for the app. The Bible text and the explanations are part of the app, so reading them does not use the internet.

On Android, the notification software the app is built with includes Google Firebase code that could be used for push messages. The app does not set it up, so it does not create a push token or send anything. The Android version also lists some standard permissions that come with its building blocks. It uses internet access (for Premium), notifications, and keeping reminders after the phone restarts. Others are listed but not used by the app, such as storage access on older Android versions and showing over other apps. The app uses no permission to collect information about you.

Why we use data, and our legal grounds

Data that stays on your phone is not collected by us. For the rest, the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) asks us to name a legal ground:

  • Selling and checking Premium (Apple or Google, and RevenueCat): to provide the subscription you asked for, which is performing our contract with you (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)).
  • Subscription statistics (from RevenueCat and the stores: how many people subscribe, renew or cancel): our legitimate interest in running and improving Premium (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • Usage statistics and crash reports from Apple and Google, if your phone settings allow them: our legitimate interest in fixing problems and improving the app (Article 6(1)(f)).
  • Answering your email: our legitimate interest in helping you and fixing problems in the app (Article 6(1)(f)), or our contract with you when your question is about your subscription.
  • Accounting records of sales, as reported to us by the stores: our legal duty under Finnish accounting law (Article 6(1)(c)).

You never have to give us personal data. Without the store receipt or purchase token we cannot check Premium, and without your email address we cannot reply to you. Everything else in the app works without either.

When you open Bible Gateway or share a verse, you send the information yourself, to a party you choose. That party decides how it uses it.

Your right to object. Where we rely on our legitimate interest (statistics, crash reports and answering your email), you can object at any time by writing to us, and we will stop unless we have compelling grounds to continue. You can also turn off the sharing of usage statistics and crash reports in your phone’s settings.

Who receives data

  • Apple, for downloads and purchases on iPhone, under the App Store privacy notice and Apple’s privacy policy.
  • Google, for downloads and purchases on Android, under Google’s privacy policy and the Google Payments privacy notice.
  • Proton AG (Switzerland), our email provider, when you email us, under its own terms and privacy policy.
  • RevenueCat, Inc., our processor for the subscription check and subscription statistics, under a data processing agreement. See RevenueCat’s privacy policy.
  • Bible Gateway, only if you open a chapter there, under its privacy notice.

RevenueCat, the one company that handles data for us, is bound by contract to protect it at least as well as this policy describes. Apple and Google act under their own responsibility as stores and payment providers, and Proton as our email provider. We may also have to share information if the law requires it, for example with the tax authorities.

Data outside the EU

RevenueCat stores data in the United States, on Amazon Web Services, and uses providers that are mainly in the United States. Our agreement with RevenueCat includes the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses to protect data sent there (RevenueCat’s data processing agreement).

Apple and Google may process data in the United States and elsewhere, as described in their own policies. Emails you send us are kept by Proton in Switzerland, which the EU recognises as giving adequate protection. Bible Gateway is in the United States; you connect to it directly when you open a chapter.

How long data is kept

  • On your phone: until you delete it in the app, clear the app’s storage, or uninstall the app. If Premium ends, your likes, notes and topics stay saved.
  • Subscription records at RevenueCat: while your subscription is active, and for up to 24 months after it ends, so we can answer questions about renewals and refunds. Then we delete them. You can ask us to delete them sooner.
  • Your emails to us: for up to 12 months after your question has been answered, unless a subscription matter or a legal claim needs them for longer. You can ask us to delete them sooner.
  • Usage statistics and crash reports: for as long as Apple or Google keep them available to us in their developer tools. They do not tell us who you are.
  • Sales records: as long as the Finnish Accounting Act requires: 10 years for the accounts and 6 years for the reports behind them, counted from the end of the financial year.

How to delete your data

Because your data is on your phone, you can delete it yourself:

  • A note: open it and choose “Delete note”, or save it empty.
  • One liked verse: tap the heart again, or double-tap it in Liked.
  • All likes: in Settings, under For you, use the “Clear … liked verses” button. Your notes are kept.
  • Suggestions from your likes: turn off “Learn from my likes”.
  • Reminders: turn Reminders off and save. All planned reminders are cancelled.
  • Everything: uninstall the app. On Android you can also go to the phone’s Settings, Apps, Stop for a Verse, Storage, and choose Clear storage. This also removes the repair copies described above.

Copies inside your own phone backups are managed in your Google or Apple account settings.

Deleting the app does not cancel a Premium subscription. Cancel it in the App Store or Google Play first (see the Terms of Use).

To have your subscription records deleted at RevenueCat, email activley@proton.me. Since we do not know your name, tell us which store you bought Premium in and roughly when, and include the order number from your receipt if you have it (on Google Play it starts with “GPA.”). If we cannot find your record from these details, we will tell you.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to ask for access to your personal data, to have it corrected or deleted, to restrict or object to its use, and to receive it in a portable form. Most of your data is only on your phone, where you can see, change and delete it yourself. For anything we or RevenueCat hold, write to activley@proton.me. We answer within one month.

Where we rely on our legitimate interest, you can object at any time, and we will stop unless we have compelling grounds to continue.

If you think we have handled your data wrongly, please tell us first so we can put it right. You can also complain to the Finnish Data Protection Ombudsman (tietosuoja.fi) or to the data protection authority where you live or work.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13. Its content, Bible verses, suits every age, and families are welcome to use it together. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children. Beyond what this page describes, the app collects no personal data from anyone. Purchases by children are controlled by the parental settings of the App Store or Google Play.

Keeping data safe

Your data is kept in the app’s private storage, which other apps cannot read, and is protected by your phone’s own security. Notes are not encrypted separately, so we recommend a screen lock. Connections to the stores and to RevenueCat are encrypted.

Changes to this policy

If the app starts handling data differently, we will update this page before the change takes effect and change the date at the top. If the change is important, we will also tell you in the app or in the store listing.
